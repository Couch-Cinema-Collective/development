# Couch Cinema Collective

**Establish a guild. Premiere your own festival. Find out who actually has the taste.**

## Overview

Couch Cinema Collective turns watching movies with friends into a festival with real structure. A guild seats 4–12 **curators** and a voting body of up to 50 **critics**. Each curator puts up one film; the guild watches them one at a time on a shared clock, reviews them anonymously, and closes the whole thing out with an awards ceremony.

Recognition isn't a made-up point score. There is exactly one award that counts — **Best of the Fest** — and it belongs to the curator who backed the winning film. Everything else is an honour: real trophies, no points.

The tone is intentionally closer to a serious film club than a game — institutional, black/white/red, PBS-style — with just enough mechanics to make putting a film up feel like something you're playing, not just a poll.

## The two chairs

The first question the app asks is which one you want.

- **Curator** — you put a single film into the festival and defend it. Think producer, not critic: you stake your name on a pick, and if it takes Best of the Fest, the win goes on your record. Seats are finite — the president sets how many, up to 12 — and they go first come first served.
- **Critic** — you're the voting body. Watch each film, write 200 characters on it, and spend three upvotes on the reviews that earned them. Room for 50 a guild, so there is effectively always a seat.

Every curator is also a critic. Curating is the extra job, never the alternative to it.

## How a festival works

1. **Programme** — The president sets a theme (a nation, a filmmaker, a genre, a movement, an era, or anything they invent) and a pace. Every curator nominates one film. Who picked what stays hidden until the lineup is drawn, so nobody plays off anyone else's choice.
2. **Screen** — Films run one at a time, in a random order, on a repeating clock: roughly a fortnight to watch, two days to write, then a 24-hour window for critics to vote on the reviews. Then the next film opens.
3. **Review** — 200 characters, anonymous until the voting window shuts. You get three upvotes per film and must spend all three, or your own review stops being eligible to receive any. Participation is the price of competing.
4. **Award** — Once every film has closed, the ballot opens. Best of the Fest settles the festival for the curators; **Voice of the People** goes to whoever earned the most upvotes across the whole run — curators included, since the writing stands on its own.

## The rhythm

The target feel is fantasy football: a weekly obligation you actually look forward to. The dashboard is built around one question — *what do I owe, and how long have I got?* — with a countdown on whatever window is currently open, the film that's on, what's coming next, and where you stand.

Miss a window and you miss that round. That's the whole eligibility system.

## How to get involved

- **Join a guild** — Get an invite link from a president, or start your own. Open festivals let strangers take a free curator seat; closed ones are invite-only by code.
- **Make your pick** — Search and nominate straight from a live film catalog: posters, cast, runtime, and where to stream each title.
- **Stay in the loop on Discord** — Nominations opening, the lineup reveal, each film's windows, and the winners all post to your guild's server automatically.
- **Explore the film wiki** — No membership required. Browse genre and movement write-ups with canonical examples — the open front door to the Collective.
